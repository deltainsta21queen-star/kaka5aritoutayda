Google Ads Search Campaign

A structured Google Ads Search campaign plan for Brewhaven Coffee Co., a fictional specialty coffee brand. The strategy separates user intent, aligns bidding with product economics, and defines how performance would be measured and optimized.

01 / Overview

Project overview

Build a scalable search campaign that supports two different purchase journeys.

Brewhaven Coffee Co. is a fictional specialty coffee brand offering two core products: a recurring coffee subscription and a one-time single-bag purchase.

PRODUCT 01

Coffee subscription

$24/month recurring subscription. This product has the potential to generate ongoing customer value beyond the first payment.

Conversion goalSubscription sign-up
PRODUCT 02

Single bag purchase

$18 one-time purchase. The acquisition target needs to account for the more limited margin and one-time transaction.

Conversion goalPurchase

The business previously relied on organic social media and influencer marketing, with no existing Google Ads account or historical conversion data. The plan uses a $3,000 monthly budget to establish a measurable foundation that can be optimized as data accumulates.

02 / Challenge

Business challenge

One brand, two products, and two different economics.

CHALLENGE 01

Different customer journeys

A customer considering a recurring subscription may have a different intent and value profile from someone buying a single bag of coffee.

CHALLENGE 02

Different acceptable acquisition costs

A subscription may justify a higher initial acquisition cost because of future payments, while a one-time purchase has a tighter margin constraint.

Strategic principle

Separate user intent, messaging, bidding and performance measurement without making the account unnecessarily complex. Conversion values and CPA targets must reflect business economics—not be treated as identical across products.

03 / Structure

Campaign structure

Start with one Search campaign and organize ad groups around user intent.

CAMPAIGN

Brewhaven Search

One campaign with a planned monthly budget of $3,000, shared conversion tracking and consistent reporting.

AD GROUP 01

Coffee subscription

Target searches from users looking for coffee subscriptions, recurring delivery and subscription plans.

AD GROUP 02

Single bag purchase

Target users with immediate purchase intent for coffee bags and specialty coffee products.

Rather than organizing the account only by product category, the structure focuses on intent and conversion economics. Each ad group can have relevant keywords, tailored ad copy, a suitable landing page and a separate CPA goal.

04 / Optimization

Two-phase bidding strategy

Begin by collecting reliable conversion data, then introduce target constraints when the campaign has enough evidence.

01

Maximize Conversions

Launch without a Target CPA so the bidding system can learn from conversion activity. Confirm conversion tracking and lead or purchase quality before relying on automated bidding.

Initial focus
  • Validate conversion tracking
  • Collect enough relevant conversion data
  • Review search terms and budget pacing
  • Check conversion quality, not only volume
02

Target CPA

Once sufficient conversion data is available, test Target CPA using product-specific targets based on unit economics and observed performance.

Illustrative CPA targets
  • Subscription: $30–$36 CPA, based on the project's assumed six-month customer lifetime value.
  • Single purchase: $6–$8 CPA, aligned with the assumed margin on an $18 purchase.
Why not Target ROAS at launch?

The proposed plan assumes each conversion within an ad group has a consistent value and prioritizes cost per acquisition. Target ROAS could be reconsidered if reliable conversion values vary and value-based optimization becomes appropriate. The CPA figures here are planning assumptions, not proven outcomes.

Keyword strategy

Balance relevance and reach, then use search-term reviews to reduce wasted spend.

MATCH TYPE 01

Negative keywords

Review goalsLess wasted spend

07 / Measurement

Key performance indicators

Measurement priorities change as the campaign moves from learning to ongoing optimization.

Learning phase

Conversion

Conversions

Track subscription sign-ups and one-time purchases using correctly configured conversion actions.

Efficiency

Cost per conversion

Monitor acquisition cost against the initial assumptions for each product.

Ad relevance

Click-through rate

Understand whether ads attract clicks from relevant searchers.

Visibility

Impression share

Review search visibility and potential lost impression share due to budget or rank.

After the learning phase

CPA vs. target

Compare actual CPA with the planned target and investigate meaningful deviations.

Conversion growth

Review conversion volume and quality over time rather than judging isolated days.

Budget allocation

Shift budget only when performance evidence and business priorities support the change.

Search-term analysis

Identify new opportunities and irrelevant queries for keyword and negative keyword updates.

Ad group comparison

Compare subscription and single-purchase performance against their different economics.

Monthly reporting

Summarize results, insights, tests and recommended optimizations for the next period.

10 / Takeaways

Key takeaways

Effective paid search depends on structure, intent, measurement and disciplined optimization.

This project demonstrates how to design a scalable Google Ads account structure, align bidding strategies with business objectives, build intent-based keyword strategies and create Responsive Search Ads with supporting assets.

It also highlights the importance of meaningful reporting: campaign data should inform the next decision, while product economics and customer value provide context for interpreting results.
