Social Media Strategy & Content Calendar

A one-month Instagram content strategy for Brew & Bloom, a fictional specialty coffee shop in Dubai. The plan connects audience research, content planning and measurable business goals—not just posting more often.

01 / Overview

Project overview

Turn business challenges into a focused, practical social media plan.

Brew & Bloom is a fictional specialty coffee shop serving single-origin coffee, espresso-based drinks, freshly baked pastries and weekend coffee cupping workshops.

The objective was to develop a one-month Instagram content strategy aligned with measurable business goals rather than simply increasing posting frequency or engagement.

Business challenges

CHALLENGE 01

Low weekday morning foot traffic

Attract nearby office professionals who may want coffee or breakfast on their commute.

CHALLENGE 02

Low workshop bookings

Build awareness and interest in weekend coffee cupping workshops and convert that interest into bookings.

Practical constraints

Organic only

No paid advertising in the proposed plan.

Limited budget

Use existing resources and simple production workflows.

Smartphone production

Plan content that can be captured with a phone.

Small team

Keep content creation realistic and repeatable.

02 / Goals

Business objectives

Every content activity supports a clear business outcome.

01

Increase weekday morning visits

Attract nearby office professionals during commuting hours with coffee, breakfast and convenience-focused content.

02

Increase weekend workshop bookings

Use educational, trust-building and promotional Instagram content to encourage workshop enquiries and reservations.

03 / Research

Audience research

Two audience groups inform the topics, formats and calls to action.

PRIMARY AUDIENCE

Nearby office professionals

People working close to the café who may buy coffee before work.

Audience characteristics
  • Ages 22–40
  • Commute to work on weekdays
  • Purchase coffee before work
  • Active Instagram users
  • Value convenience, quality and café atmosphere
SECONDARY AUDIENCE

Specialty coffee enthusiasts

People interested in coffee culture, learning and memorable café experiences.

Audience characteristics
  • Interested in coffee origins and brewing
  • Enjoy café experiences
  • Likely to attend workshops
  • Engage with educational coffee content
04 / Content plan

Four content pillars

The proposed mix gives each pillar a distinct role in the marketing strategy.

PILLAR 0140%

Workshop promotion

Increase awareness and bookings for weekend coffee workshops.

  • Workshop previews
  • Previous attendee testimonials
  • Limited-seat reminders
  • Booking calls to action
PILLAR 0230%

Weekday traffic campaigns

Encourage weekday morning visits from nearby professionals.

  • Coffee and breakfast promotions
  • Morning-routine Reels
  • Workday-focused messaging
  • Convenience-driven content
PILLAR 0320%

Community and behind the scenes

Build trust by showing the people and atmosphere behind the brand.

  • Barista introductions
  • Daily café operations
  • Customer interactions
  • Café atmosphere
PILLAR 0410%

Coffee education

Position the café as a specialty coffee destination and support workshop interest.

  • Coffee origin stories
  • Bean comparisons
  • Brewing tips
  • Coffee facts

Percentages represent the planned content mix, not measured campaign results.

05 / Journey

Customer journey strategy

Content is sequenced to introduce the brand, build interest and trust, then invite the audience to take action.

MONDAY · 01

Awareness

Introduce the café and reach new people with useful, location-relevant content.

TUESDAY · 02

Interest

Show the experience, products and atmosphere to encourage curiosity.

WEDNESDAY · 03

Education

Share coffee knowledge that provides value and builds credibility.

THURSDAY · 04

Consideration

Explain the workshop experience and answer likely questions.

FRIDAY · 05

Conversion

Use a clear booking call to action to encourage reservations.

08 / Measurement

KPI framework

Business outcomes are the primary indicators of success. Social metrics help explain how content contributes to those outcomes.

Business KPIs

Primary KPI

Workshop bookings

Track the number of reservations generated for weekend workshops.

Primary KPI

Workshop capacity utilization

Compare booked seats with available workshop capacity.

Primary KPI

Morning revenue

Monitor revenue during the targeted weekday morning period.

Primary KPI

Morning transaction volume

Measure changes in the number of morning purchases.

Marketing KPIs

Reach

Unique accounts exposed to content.

Reel views

Video visibility and viewing activity.

Story engagement

Replies, sticker interactions and other Story activity.

Link clicks

Visits to booking or information links.

Saves and shares

Signals that content is useful or worth passing on.

Direct messages

Enquiries and conversations that may lead to a visit or booking.

09 / Attribution

How impact would be tracked

Use several measurement methods to connect social content with customer actions, rather than relying on engagement alone.

01

Customer self-reporting

Ask customers how they heard about the café or workshop.

02

Instagram link tracking

Use trackable links for profile, Story and campaign traffic.

03

Workshop enquiry tracking

Record enquiries and bookings that reference social media.

04

UTM parameters

Label campaign links to identify traffic sources in analytics.

05

Booking analytics

Review website or booking-platform actions where available.

06

Regular reporting

Compare weekly activity and monthly business outcomes.

12 / Conclusion

Key takeaways

This project demonstrates how to build a social media strategy around business objectives instead of vanity metrics. Audience research, content pillars and a structured customer journey make each publication serve a clear purpose.

Combining a realistic content calendar with business KPIs and attribution methods creates a framework that can be reviewed, measured and improved over time.
